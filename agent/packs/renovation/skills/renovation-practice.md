# Renovation practice

## Typical order of works (adjust to the scope)
1. Decisions and measurements → 2. Permits/approvals (if structural, façade or shared utilities) → 3. Protection and demolition →
4. Rough utilities (plumbing, electrics, ventilation, heating) → 5. Walls and screeds (let them cure — days/weeks) →
6. Waterproofing in wet rooms → 7. Windows/doors as needed → 8. Plaster/levelling, drying → 9. Flooring base and tiling →
10. Ceilings and painting prep → 11. Final electrics (sockets, lighting), plumbing fixtures → 12. Flooring/skirting, paint finish →
13. Furniture, curtains, cleaning. Order deliveries so the long-lead items (windows, kitchens, custom doors) are ordered first.

## Money
Contingency 10–20 % (older buildings and unknowns → upper end). Waste: tiles 10–15 % (diagonal 15–20 %), flooring 7–10 %, paint 5–10 %.
Never plan to the last cent of the ceiling; keep a reserve for what the opened wall reveals. Price per m² is a sanity check, not an estimate.

## Measuring
Measure each wall at two heights and each room diagonally to find out of square; record ceiling height at several points, door/window
openings with depth, and where pipes/sockets/switches are. Photograph every wall straight-on with a reference object (a tape measure).
Write numbers down in the project's Measurements note — agents use only what is written.

## Professionals and permits (say this plainly, every time it applies)
Load-bearing or structural changes, gas, mains electrics and consumer-unit work, fire/escape routes, façade/window openings changes and
waterproofing in wet rooms need qualified people and often a permit or approval of the building's management. Plan and budget for them;
do not give do-it-yourself steps that replace them.

## Briefing with photos
One photo per wall/feature, straight-on and lit; add a one-line description (room, wall, side). For "what would this look like" questions
attach 2–3 photos from different corners plus a reference of the style wanted.

## Decision hygiene
Decide big surfaces → colours → lighting → furniture; lock choices in the brief so later work does not contradict earlier work.
Keep a list of "decisions owed" with a date by which the delivery lead time makes them urgent.
