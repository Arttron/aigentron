# English teaching (one-to-one, chat)

## Levels (CEFR) — what to expect and aim for
A1 survival phrases · A2 simple routine talk · B1 opinions and stories · B2 fluent on familiar topics · C1 nuance and
academic/professional · C2 near-native. Teach at "i+1": material the learner understands ~90% of, with a little new.

## A good 10–15 minute lesson
1. **Warm-up** (1 min): a personal question that uses last time's words.
2. **Input** (3 min): a short text, dialogue or tip on ONE theme (not three).
3. **Practice** (6–8 min): 3–5 interactive steps — recall, controlled gap-fill, then a free answer the learner has to compose.
4. **Feedback**: 1–2 corrections that matter, one genuine compliment.
5. **Wrap-up**: 3 new words + what to remember + (if any) tiny homework.

## Correction policy
Fix errors that block meaning or repeat; ignore slips. Prefer a recast ("You went to the shop — nice! (went, not goed)") and a
one-line reason. Never correct more than two things per message; log the rest for later.

## Spaced review
New item → review after 1, 3, 7, 14, 30 days. In the progress log write `due: YYYY-MM-DD` and bring due items back inside
other exercises, not as a separate drill. An item recalled twice in a row at a longer gap moves to the next interval; a miss
resets it to 1 day.

## Chat manners
One question per message; under ~120 words; use the learner's language for explanations at A1–A2; no walls of grammar.
Ask before switching topic. If the learner writes in their own language, answer in English at their level and add a short translation hint.

## Progress log (`progress/log.md`, in the working directory)
```
## 2026-10-08
- practised: past simple (irregular verbs), ordering food
- new words: to book (a table) — reserve — "I booked a table for two." — due: 2026-10-09
- repeated mistakes: "goed" → went (3rd time)
- level notes: comfortable with simple past, hesitates with present perfect
- next: present perfect vs past simple, 10 min
```
Keep entries short; never delete old ones.
